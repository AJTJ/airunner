## Claims that should not be repeated

1. **"Anti-hack prompting cuts impossible-task hacking by over 9× (Claude Opus 4)."** The 9× is arithmetic on the withdrawn 5% figure. With the corrected 19% (Opus 4.1 addendum, Table 5.B fn.3) it is ~2.7× (Opus 4), ~7× (Sonnet 4); on Opus 4.5 the same prompt gives 55% → 35% (~1.6×). Say "prompting reduced it 1.6–7× depending on model and eval version" — or, better, cite the two tables. (Rows 56–57.)
2. **"The Claude 4 system card, §6, Table 6.2.A, footnote 29 reports 51% / 19%."** Wrong document: the May 2025 card says 47%/5% and 45%/10%; 51%/19%/7% and the erratum are in the **Opus 4.1 addendum (Aug 2025), Table 5.B, footnote 3**. (Row 56.)
3. **"CLI-only runs were 5.0× to 28× cheaper than MCP" / "the one controlled interface comparison found equal completion at 5–28× the cost."** The 5–28× compares two no-MCP *scaffoldings* against five MCP-capable scaffoldings on CLI runs alone; the strictly paired MCP-vs-CLI ratios span 0.43×–29×. The defensible interface claims are: MCP is unnecessary for the six git operations, failure *frequency* was equal, and MCP failures wasted 12.9% of spend vs 2.2%. (Row 42.)
4. **"No replication or follow-up to the METR RCT exists."** METR's own late-2025 study (57 developers, 800+ tasks; point estimates −18% and −4% completion time, CIs crossing zero, self-declared unreliable) was published 24 Feb 2026. Quote the 2025 result with the 2026 caveat. (Row 32.)
5. **Brown et al. MATH figures "79.8% → 95.3% coverage; 38.7% → 39.8% selection."** Those are v1; v3 (Dec 2024) says 82.9% → 98.44% and 40.50% → 41.41%. (Row 34.)
6. **"Silo-Bench and 2606.00655 are neither peer-reviewed."** Silo-Bench is ACL 2026 Main. (Row 51.)
7. **"Salas et al. 2001 found mixed evidence on behaviour change."** They report positive behaviour change; only the organisational-safety level is unascertained. (Row 24.)
8. **"Rigby & Bird: review latency in days not weeks."** Median completion is 15–21 hours; first response within hours. (Row 19.)
9. **"Every cell drops" (Huang Table 3).** One cell is flat; none rises above baseline — say "no cell improves". (Row 33.)
10. **The swebench.com "top ≈ 79.2%" leaderboard snapshot** — dated; do not quote without re-fetching. (Row 48.)
11. **Digest §4.1 "Nagappan 86.2%/84% did not survive"** should itself not be repeated as a claim about the *numbers*: they are in Table 3 of the paper (see next section). What did not survive was an earlier fetch's fabricated table.

## Gaps worth new research

1. **The anti-hack prompt is decaying as an intervention** (9× claimed → 1.6× on Opus 4.5). Whether `CLAUDE.md`-style "tell me if the task is impossible" rules still move the needle on current models is measurable locally: seed the queue with a few known-impossible beads and count honest stops vs plausible completions. ImpossibleBench (2510.20270) and CapCode (2606.07379) give ready designs.
2. **Interface cost is unresolved, scaffolding cost is not.** Alier Forment et al. found paired MCP/CLI ratios from 0.43× to 29× and agents ignoring their assigned interface. For this runtime the actionable number is wasted-spend-on-failure (12.9% vs 2.2%), which is cheap to instrument per run.
3. **Ownership granularity.** Greiler et al. show directory-level ownership predicts defects better than file-level; Rahman & Devanbu show per-file specialised experience matters. Nobody has tested whether *agent* worktree partitions should follow directories (Conway) or the bottleneck (TOC) — Q7 in the note — and the Microsoft data now suggest directory.
4. **METR-style measurement of the operator's own throughput perception** in a multi-agent setting: METR's 2026 note says timing becomes unmeasurable once developers run several agents concurrently — exactly this project's regime. A local design that logs claim-to-merge wall-clock per bead sidesteps that.
5. **Debate helps detection, hurts generation** (2606.02866: −1.6 to −15.5pp on generation, +27.4pp F1 on error detection, with a code-executing Critic). That conditional maps onto "reviewer agent as detector, not fixer" and is untested here.
6. **The §4 checklist literature still lacks a post-2014 update in the note**: later WHO-checklist meta-analyses (e.g. Bergs et al. 2014; Abbott et al. 2018 pooled analysis) and the I-PASS multi-site follow-ups (Starmer et al. 2023, 32 hospitals) would settle whether the "pathway-shaped protocols work, mandated checkpoints don't" reading survives — not fetched in this pass.
7. **Fagan's own numbers** and the SmartBear/Cisco parameters remain un-re-verified from primary text; low priority given the note already labels them vendor evidence.
