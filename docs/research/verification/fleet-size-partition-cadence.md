# Verification: fleet size, partitioning, integration cadence

**Date:** 2026-08-18. **Author:** ajtj (with Claude Fable 5).
**Scope.** The externally-cited claims that carry the fleet-size, partitioning and integration-cadence arguments in `private/research/adopter-notes/notes/` — `does-the-prior-art-transfer.md`, `research-actions.md`, `fleet-run-structure.md`, and the externally-cited parts of `overnight-fleet-retrospective.md` / `round-2026-08-15-evening-retrospective.md` — plus the "claims that did not survive" list in `adopter-research-corpus.md` §4.1.
**Method.** For each load-bearing claim: quote briefly with `file:line`; locate the primary source (arXiv abstract / DOI landing / PDF / vendor page); fetch it and check numbers, population and conditions; verdict CONFIRMED / MISQUOTED / OVERSTATED / UNVERIFIABLE / SUPERSEDED; where the claim bears on a live decision (how many agents, worktree isolation, partition unit, integration cadence) add 1–2 newer 2026 sources. Fetches were done from a clean session on 2026-08-18; nothing below is copied from the notes' own verification.
**Not in scope.** Internal (own-run) measurements — merge counts, idle minutes, CV 1.38 — are the repo's own data and were not re-derived.

## Claims that should not be repeated (as currently phrased)

1. **"We are 'Independent' — 58% overhead; an orchestrator layer costs 5–9× what we run now"** (`research-actions.md:365-367`). Kim's Independent topology is n redundant solvers of one task feeding an aggregator; the overhead metric is inter-agent messages per task. One-agent-per-bead in worktrees is n single-agent runs (overhead 0 per task by Kim's definition). The topology ratios do not price a non-solving coordinator.
2. **"File overlap OR 6.13 vs branch lifetime OR 1.04–1.09 — two orders of magnitude / a factor of six"** (`fleet-run-structure.md:43-44`, `:401`, `:1850`; `overnight-fleet-retrospective.md:318-320`; research-actions STOP list). The 6.13 is for a binary variable, the 1.04–1.09 per SD of Z-standardized duration; the ratio is not meaningful. Say instead, in Dias's words: modularity and size dominate timing, and duration's "effect is not as relevant". Also "common slice" ≠ "same file".
3. **"Do not quote a population figure for Dias"** — retire the warning; the population is 73,504 scenarios / 125 projects (100 Rails + 25 Django); 182,273 / 80 / 8 languages is Menezes et al. 2021.
4. **"Read-only comprehension fans out and is measured to pay (+29.8pp)"** (`research-actions.md:369-372`). Division of labour alone is +7.2pp at ~6× cost; the +29.8 needs AgentRadio's passive-awareness messaging channel.
5. **"STORM: soft isolation and worktree isolation are statistically indistinguishable"** — the paper says "similar"; no test reported.
6. **"BSP's own authors say barriers should be used as sparingly as possible"** without the next sentence ("not nearly as inherently expensive as … folklore").
7. **"Li et al. tested n ∈ {2,4,6,8}"** — they tested 1..8. The conclusion (8 is the top of range) is unchanged.
8. **"Nagappan 86.2%/84% did not survive"** — over-corrected; the fabricated artefact was a table, the numbers are in the paper (87%/84%).
9. **The CAID-vs-STORM "unresolved contradiction"** should be restated: both show worktree ≈ soft isolation on Commit0-Lite; the disagreement is whether either beats a *strong* single agent, and the single-agent baseline moved 53.1 → 66.4 between the two papers with a model upgrade.

## Claims strengthened

- **Kim et al.'s core numbers** (260 configs; 58/263/285/515%; β = −0.236, p = 0.004; ≈0.45 boundary; "prohibitively thin beyond 3–4 agents") are verbatim, and research-actions' three caveats (Fig. 5 only, Flash optimum at 7, 45% derived) are also verbatim.
- **CAID's 92.1% / 44.3% simpy result** is no longer single-sourced — it is in Figure 6 and the text; the "Do NOT assign multiple engineers to modify the same file" prompt is in Appendix A. File-level ownership as the partition unit is the best-supported partition claim in the corpus (CAID + Claim Plane + Co-Coder + CooperBench's 33.2% work-overlap failures).
- **Brun 2011's "one third of conflicts invisible to git"** — the paper contains both phrasings; the conservative one is arithmetically consistent. 20% devolve / 93% from clean state confirmed. Together with Kasi & Sarma (test failures 5.6–35% on textually clean merges) this is the strongest cadence argument in the corpus: merge on arrival, *verify the merged result*.
- **The reviewer bottleneck** (2/L) now has 2026 field evidence: Habituation at the Gate (latency +3.5×, scrutiny −22%), plus vendor telemetry.
- **Cognition's two posts** are quoted accurately; the first has no measurements; the second says "writes stay single-threaded" and "still hold for parallel-writer swarms" verbatim.
- **Reinertsen principle numbering** — both passes agree with the mirror; not a real dispute.
- **Systems-coordinator citations** (Caladan 75% / 560k reallocations / 10 µs; Shenango "unique vantage point" / 60k; Shinjuku 6.6×) are all verbatim; the duty-cycle transfer caveat is the note's and is correct.

## Gaps worth new research

1. **The Kim β<0 regime applied to one-agent-per-bead is untested.** Every budget-matched scaling result (Kim, Li, Tran & Kiela) has n agents on *one* task. The construct that matches the fleet — n independent tasks, one agent each, serialized landing — is only touched by CooperBench (2 features), CAID/STORM (delegated subtasks) and Xu/AgenticFlict (field PRs). A clean experiment: same bead queue, N ∈ {1,2,4}, measure merged-and-verified beads per human-review-hour, not per agent-hour.
2. **Solo baseline drift.** CAID→STORM shows the solo baseline rising 13pp with one model step while the worktree arm rose 5pp. Re-run the fleet's own single-agent success-rate measurement (E1 in research-actions) after every model upgrade; the fleet-size decision is a function of it.
3. **AgentRadio-style passive awareness on read-only beads** vs "one message only" — direct contradiction with CooperBench in the corpus, resolvable by task shape (interdependent subtasks of one question vs. independent features). Worth a small A/B on the fleet's read-only beads before "route read-only to fan-out" is acted on.
4. **Review latency L and reviewer habituation** — still unmeasured locally; 2606.22721 gives the instrument (approval rate, inline-comment effort, latency by reviewer experience).
5. **Whether "common slice" (Dias) or "same file" (CAID) is the right partition unit for this repo** — Dias explicitly says slice-disjoint work still conflicts on config and cross-slice files; the repo's own CHANGELOG/openapi/spine-file data agrees. A per-file "shared spine" list with an enforced no-write rule is the cheapest test.
6. **A modern Dias-style regression on agent PRs** — AgenticFlict has the population (142K PRs); nobody has yet fitted file-overlap vs. branch-lifetime odds ratios on agent-authored PRs.
